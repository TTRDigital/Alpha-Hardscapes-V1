# Alpha Hardscapes website redesign: research report

**Prepared for:** Kia (TTR Digital Marketing) · **Client:** Alpha Hardscapes, Cheshire, CT · **Date:** 23 November 2026

This report summarises the analysis of **105 websites** (the full per-site write-ups are in `docs/research/`), what the best of them do, how the agency's inspiration reference (lionscreekconstruction.com) compares, and the design direction that the new site implements.

| Set | Sites | File |
|---|---|---|
| Lions Creek Outdoor (inspiration reference, analysed in depth) | 1 | `research/lions-creek.md` |
| Northeast and Mid-Atlantic hardscape / design-build contractors (MA, RI, NH, VT, ME, NY, NJ, PA, MD, VA) | 22 | `research/northeast-contractors.md` |
| National and international award-winning design-build firms (NALP, HNA, Houzz, UK, AU) | 20 | `research/national-design-build.md` |
| Pool, outdoor-living, premium custom builders and deck builders (Lions Creek's own league) | 20 | `research/outdoor-living-builders.md` |
| Design showcase (paver manufacturers, landscape architects, Webflow/Framer galleries) and the highest-converting home-services sites | 20 | `research/showcase-and-conversion.md` |
| Connecticut competitors (Cheshire, Southington, New Haven, Hartford and Fairfield Counties) | 23 | `research/connecticut-competitors.md` |

Each site was scored 1 to 10 for **design quality** and separately for **lead-generation effectiveness**, with homepage structure, portfolio pattern, typography and palette, trust signals, CTAs, navigation and mobile notes recorded. Fetches were text and HTML based (fonts, CSS and scripts were read from the markup), so colour and motion notes are inferred rather than seen.

---

## 1. Headline findings

1. **Beauty and conversion did not correlate.** The best-looking sites (Hobbs Inc., Harold Leidner, Mariani, Landscape Creations of RI, Pristine Acres, Glengate) score 3 to 6 on lead-gen: footer-only phone numbers, "begin an inquiry" exclusivity, no reviews, no forms on the home page. The highest-converting sites (Groff, Hinkle Outdoor Living, Decks by Kiefer, Bahler Brothers, West Shore Home) are visually average. **Nobody in the 105 does both well.** That is the opening for Alpha: take the layout and type discipline from the first group and the conversion stack from the second.
2. **Lions Creek is not a home builder. It is a direct competitor.** Its page title is "Lions Creek Outdoor | Custom Pools and Outdoor Living in Connecticut" (New Haven / Wallingford; pools, outdoor kitchens, masonry). It sells $250k+ gunite pool "estates"; its site is built to filter for that buyer. Alpha's audience is broader and phone-driven, so the inspiration has to be translated, not copied (section 3).
3. **The Connecticut field is weak.** Of 23 local competitors, about a third are dated template sites, a third are competent templates and only a handful (Bahler Brothers, Glengate, Hoffman, Pete's, AQ Masonry, Elite) are modern. Nobody in New Haven County publishes real project case studies with town names; 60% show no Home Improvement Contractor (HIC) number; only one (Bahler) offers financing; only two (Bahler, J&H) have a sticky mobile call bar.
4. **The conversion patterns that recur on every high-scoring site** (section 2) are cheap to build and almost absent locally: phone in the header and a sticky mobile bar, one repeated first-person CTA, a short estimate form above the fold, a Google rating with a count near the hero, a named 4-5 step process, town pages with real local content, project cards with town and materials.
5. **Before/after sliders are "best practice" everywhere and used almost nowhere.** Zero of 21 Northeast sites, 3 of 20 national winners and 1 of 23 CT sites surface one. Alpha has before/after photography already; it is the cheapest differentiator in the set.
6. **Typography: serif display + clean sans is the premium signature** (Playfair + Inter at Lions Creek, Spectral + Archivo at Hobbs, Mercury + Gotham at Southern Botanical, Larken + Inter at Meadowlark); the mid-market hardscape signature is all-geometric-sans (Montserrat, Poppins). Palettes default to deep green + charcoal + white; the stand-outs use warm cream/sand/olive neutrals and let the photography carry the colour.

## 2. What the best of the 105 do (patterns, ranked by how often they appear on the top-scoring sites)

### Layout and structure
- **9 to 11 homepage sections, one idea each, generous padding.** Premium sites run 6 to 9 (Hobbs, Anthony Wilder, Lions Creek); lead-gen templates run 14 to 17 (Barrett, Keystone, Kiefer) and feel like a scroll marathon.
- **Eyebrow + headline + one sentence + one CTA per band** (Belgard's "THE ART OF WARMTH / Fire pits and fireplaces… / Explore fire features"). Directly reusable for service sections.
- **Hero formula of the top converters:** positioning line, proof line (rating + review count), one primary CTA plus a visible phone number, 3 to 4 trust bullets (free estimates, insured, years, financing). Northface, Capstone, Premier Pavers, Absolute Landscapes and Hinkle all follow it.
- **Stats band under the hero** (years, projects, square feet of pavers, rating): Premier Pavers, Southern Botanical, Outside Landscape Group.
- **Navigation: 5 to 6 top-level items with the CTA as a button.** Mega-menus (Platinum, Barrett, Deck Remodelers) help SEO but cost polish; the long service list belongs in a Services hub and the footer.

### How projects are shown
- **Cards with name + town + one-sentence description, filters that mirror the services, and a page per project.** Milosi (Pavers & Patios / Retaining Walls / Firepits), Heritage Pools (filter by material, size, feature, town, "projects near [town]"), Monello and Sponzilli ("[Title] – [Town, ST]"). Image-only grids (Gibbs, Frisella, Drakeley, Alpha's current gallery) score lowest for both design and SEO.
- **Project pages with a story:** Meadowlark's Snapshot / Product Selections / What Changed, Mariani's role / materials / designers, Keystone's investment range and spec list, Hobbs's chaptered images with credits. Common anatomy: hero, 75 to 200 words, 8 to 30 photos, materials list, before/after, testimonial, prev/next, CTA.
- **Captions as specification** ("Cheshire, CT – Bluestone patio, Unilock Rivercrest wall, gas fire pit – 2025"), borrowed from Eldorado Stone and Unilock galleries.

### Typography, colour, imagery
- Serif display + humanist sans body, with small uppercase tracked eyebrows (and occasionally mono numerals) for labels. Never more than three families.
- Neutral chrome with one accent confined to buttons and eyebrows. Warm neutrals (cream, sand, olive, charcoal) stand out from the green-and-white default.
- Dusk / "lights on" photography with fire features, drone aerials, consistent 3:2 and 4:3 crops, separate mobile crops.
- Motion only where it serves content: before/after drag, blur-up image loading, quiet scroll reveals. Heavy motion sites were also the heaviest pages.

### Trust signals
- A Google rating **with a count** in the hero and above every form (only 6 of 23 CT sites do this).
- Credentials shown once, legibly, with a one-line explainer: HIC number, ICPI/CMHA, manufacturer authorised-contractor badges (Techo-Pro, Unilock Authorized, Belgard), insurance, warranty years.
- A named, numbered process (3 to 8 steps) on every site scoring 7+ on lead-gen. Specific commitments beat adjectives: "same-day reply before 5 pm", "timelines and budgets guaranteed", "free 1-hour consultation".
- Owner visibility (Pete's, Elite, AQ, Bahler, Gohlke's 80 staff) correlates with the better converting sites.

### CTAs and forms
- One first-person label repeated 5 to 7 times ("Get My Free Estimate", "Request a Free Estimate"). Generic "Contact us" and "Learn more" heroes score lowest.
- Phone number clickable in the header, repeated near every form, and a **sticky bottom bar on mobile with at most two buttons** (Call + Free Estimate, 48px). Only Decks by Kiefer, Bahler and J&H actually ship one.
- Estimate form above the fold: 4 to 5 fields after a zero-typing qualifier (project type, town). Never ask "how did you hear", full address or budget first. Multi-step beats single-step in published tests (+18 to +21%).
- Financing in the value-prop strip with its own page (8 of 10 high converters; only Bahler in CT).
- Evidence on phone vs form: Invoca's 2026 benchmark (60M calls) finds 38% of answered home-services calls are leads and 45% of those convert; the "10 to 15x" multiple seen in blogs is unverified. Make calling the fastest path on mobile, keep the form for evening desktop research, offer a call-back.

### Local SEO
- Town pages with real local content (permits, soils, neighbourhoods, projects in that town). The failure mode is the name-swap template (AQ Masonry, Northface, Brookscapes); the success mode is Southview's map + 15 city links and Alpine's 14 localised pages.
- Server-rendered HTML with LocalBusiness, Service, FAQ, Review and Breadcrumb schema. JS-only SPAs (Lions Creek) and heavy Elementor builds lose here.
- Question-led service page headings that map to search queries ("Do pavers crack in winter?", "What is thinstone?").

## 3. Lions Creek vs. the best of the 100

### What Lions Creek is
A Lovable-built Vite/React single-page app on Vercel: dark navy (#0b1728) with a single gold accent (#c9a65e), Playfair Display headlines with an italic-gold "last phrase" device, Inter body at 14 to 16px light, 96 to 160px section padding, hairline dividers, square corners. A muted 100vh hero video (12.8 MB desktop / 3.4 MB mobile), a three-step Design / Permitting / Construction story, two featured project videos (50 MB each), a 28-image unlabeled masonry portfolio, five hand-rolled before/after sliders named by town, question-led service pages, and one CTA ("Schedule a Site Visit", 13 times) that actually opens a GoHighLevel "intro call" calendar. No reviews, ratings, awards, licence, years or warranty anywhere; the phone appears only in the footer (three different numbers across the site and its schema); no sticky mobile bar; all body content needs JavaScript.

### What to borrow as patterns (and where the 100 do it better)
| Lions Creek pattern | Verdict | Best-in-class version among the 100 |
|---|---|---|
| Typographic restraint: one serif display, one sans, one accent, hairline rules | **Borrow** | Hobbs Inc. (Spectral + Archivo + mono labels), Meadowlark (Larken + Inter), Southview (Lustria + Red Hat Text) |
| Process as a trust device (animated 10-step timeline, design-only vs build-only comparison) | **Borrow, shorten to 5 steps with the first touch named** | Designs By Sundown (Connect, Site Visit, Planning, Execution, Care), Groff (4 steps + 24-hour reply) |
| "One team, one contract, one point of contact" narrative | **Borrow** | Hobbs ("One team · One line of accountability"), Pete's Landscaping (base-prep storytelling) |
| Question-led H2s on service pages with Service schema | **Borrow** | Hinkle Outdoor Living (FAQs with $/sq ft), AQ Masonry (permit and cost FAQs per town) |
| Before/after drag sliders named by town | **Borrow and put them on project pages, not an orphan page** | Anthony Wilder ("View Before" toggle in galleries), Lifescape (Before & After filter) |
| Fixed-ratio square image cards and consistent per-page CTA bands | **Borrow** | Belgard's eyebrow/headline/CTA band system |
| Mobile-specific hero video encode with poster | **Borrow the discipline, drop the video** | Hobbs paused slideshow; Southview dusk stills |

### What to do better than Lions Creek
1. **Social proof.** Lions Creek shows none. Alpha has ten 5-star Google reviews with names; the new site puts the rating in the hero, on every service page, above every form, and on a dedicated Reviews page with Review schema.
2. **Lead capture.** Replace the single calendar-booking funnel and iframe form with a native 5-field estimate form in the hero and the sidebar of every service, town and project page, a header phone, and a sticky Call / Free estimate bar on phones (none of the 20 builder sites, and only two CT sites, have one).
3. **Portfolio with context.** Replace the 28-photo dump with project pages carrying title, town, services, materials, summary, photo set and before/after, filterable by service, each indexable.
4. **Local SEO.** Lions Creek's H1 ("Not a backyard. A private resort.") contains no service or place. Alpha's headings name patios, walkways, walls and Cheshire; county and town pages carry real local copy; everything is server-rendered with business, service, FAQ, breadcrumb and review schema.
5. **Performance.** No 800 KB JS bundle, no 13 MB hero video, no 50 MB project videos: static HTML, optimised WebP photos with real `srcset`, system-level lazy loading and a light theme that keeps text contrast.
6. **Consistency.** One phone number, one legal name, one address everywhere, matching the schema.

### What does not fit Alpha Hardscapes
- **The dark navy theme.** Pavers, bluestone and plantings read better on a warm, light canvas; dark pages also fight the white third-party widgets. Alpha's brand is black and lime green; the new site uses a warm stone off-white with ink-black sections and lime only for actions and eyebrows.
- **Luxury-pool "private resort" gating.** "Begin an inquiry" copy, no prices, no "free estimate" filters out the New Haven and Hartford County homeowner who wants a patio or wall and expects a phone number. Alpha's words are "free on-site estimate", "written quote", "we reply within one business day".
- **Video-first hero and heavy scroll motion.** They delay the first CTA by two seconds on a site whose leads come from mobile searches.
- **Client-side rendering.** Local SEO needs crawlable HTML, town pages and schema.
- **Statewide "Connecticut" copy.** Alpha is a Cheshire company; the site says so in the first line and builds out from the real service radius.

## 4. Design direction implemented in the new site

**Original, not a copy.** The build takes the discipline of the editorial tier and the conversion stack of the lead-gen tier, on Alpha's own brand.

- **Palette:** warm stone off-white (#f8f6f1) page, ink black (#141614) sections and footer, brand lime (#adce39) only for buttons, eyebrows and highlights, moss green (#2f4a2a) for links and icons. Photography carries the colour.
- **Type:** Fraunces (variable serif with optical sizing) for display, Manrope for UI and body, 11px tracked uppercase eyebrows. Three families maximum, two used.
- **Home page (10 sections):** hero with headline, trust bullets, Google badge, primary CTA + phone, and an estimate card on desktop; stats band; six service cards; six project cards; founder + "why us" six-up; dark five-step process; before/after slider; three reviews with rating badge; service-area map with county cards and town links; five FAQs; CTA band. Sticky Call / Free estimate bar on phones.
- **Service pages:** photo hero with breadcrumbs, CTA, phone and rating; intro; four highlights; question-led body; materials chips; photo grid with lightbox; FAQs with FAQ schema; sticky estimate card and two reviews in the sidebar; related projects; "often built together" services; Service schema.
- **Projects:** filterable grid (filters mirror services); project pages with town, materials, completed date, summary, before/after slider, photo gallery, prev/next and a CTA card. Fifteen projects seeded from the live gallery; towns and materials are fields the client fills in.
- **Service areas:** three county pages and seven town pages with real local copy, highlights, towns list, projects, map, FAQs, local estimate form and nearby-town links. Adding a town is a form in the CMS.
- **Trust stack:** rating + count in hero and above forms; "fully insured" with a licence line field ready for the CT HIC number; founder on the home and About pages; process; 15 FAQs with financing and payment answers; schema everywhere.
- **Conversion stack:** one CTA label ("Get a Free Estimate"); header phone; sticky mobile bar; 5-field form (name, phone, email, town, project type) with honeypot, consent line and a response promise; thank-you page; leads delivered to GoHighLevel and/or email.
- **Performance and SEO:** Next.js static generation, WebP at three sizes, AVIF/WebP optimisation, trailing-slash URLs matching the live blog, 301 map for every old URL, sitemap, robots, canonical and Open Graph on every page.

## 5. Gaps and recommendations for the client (not in the build)

1. **HIC registration number.** Connecticut requires it on all advertising. Add it in Site settings → Licence line (`Registered CT Home Improvement Contractor HIC.#… · Fully insured`).
2. **Review count.** Add the Google review count to Site settings once confirmed; the badge shows "on Google" until then.
3. **Towns and materials on projects.** The old gallery had neither. Ten minutes in the Studio per project.
4. **Manufacturer badges and warranty.** If Alpha holds Techo-Pro, Unilock Authorized or ICPI status, or offers a written workmanship warranty, add them: only AQ Masonry and Bahler show any locally.
5. **Financing.** A Hearth/Wisetack link would make Alpha the second CT competitor to offer it.
6. **Dusk photography.** One evening shoot of a fire-pit patio with lighting would lift the hero above every competitor in the county.
7. **Pricing guide and permit FAQs.** A Connecticut cost guide (the blog already has cost posts) and "Do I need a permit in Cheshire?" answers on town pages are unclaimed territory.
8. **Google Ads landing pages.** `/1000-off/`, `/20pct-off/` and `/optin/` now redirect to `/contact/`; re-point the ads or rebuild the offers in the CMS before launch.
